## Overview

The 'kidney thieves' or 'kidney heist' urban legend is one of the most heavily documented modern American urban legends. In its canonical form a businessman or traveler meets an attractive stranger at a bar (most commonly in New Orleans, Las Vegas, or another convention city), accepts a drink, blacks out, and awakens hours later in a hotel bathtub filled with ice; a phone is placed beside the tub and a note instructs them to call 911 immediately, where they are told that one or both of their kidneys have been surgically removed and almost certainly sold on the black market. The legend was tracked from at least 1991 by folklorists and journalists, and from the mid-1990s by Snopes, the National Kidney Foundation, and law-enforcement agencies. It spread explosively as one of the first major chain-email legends in 1996–97. Despite intense investigation by police, transplant medicine professionals, and journalists, no documented case matching the legend has ever been substantiated; it is now a textbook example in folklore studies of an organ-theft 'contemporary legend' (Jan Harold Brunvand). It is distinct from the real, separately documented problem of organ trafficking (e.g., the 2008 Gurgaon kidney scandal in India), which involves consenting-but-coerced or deceived donors, not surreptitious surgical kidnapping.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Medical impossibility** _(National Kidney Foundation, American Society of Transplantation)_

Transplant surgeons and nephrologists note the legend is not medically plausible: kidney harvesting requires HLA matching, sterile operative conditions, and cold-chain logistics far beyond what a hotel room could supply, and no organ is wasted in the manner the legend implies.

### Skeptical

**Contemporary legend (folkloristic)** _(Jan Harold Brunvand, Véronique Campion-Vincent, Snopes)_

The bathtub-of-ice story is a contemporary legend whose endurance is sociological: it encodes anxieties about travel, sexual strangers, and the medical commodification of the body.
